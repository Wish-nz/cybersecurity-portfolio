**Understanding the Principle of Least Privilege**

- The principle of least privilege (PoLP) dictates that users should only have the minimum access and authorization necessary to perform their tasks.
- This principle is fundamental to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) triad of information security.

**Implementing Least Privilege**

- Implementing PoLP involves determining who the user is and how much access they need to specific resources.
- User accounts can be categorized as guest, user, service, or privileged accounts, each requiring a baseline access level.

**Auditing Account Privileges**

- Routine auditing of user accounts is essential to maintain security and prevent "privilege creep," where users accumulate more access than needed.
- Common auditing approaches include usage audits (reviewing resource access), privilege audits (assessing alignment of roles and access), and account change audits (monitoring changes for suspicious activity).

Fundamental Security Principles

- **Principle of Least Privilege:** This principle dictates that a user should only be granted the minimum level of access and authorization necessary to complete their tasks.
- **Separation of Duties:** This principle ensures that responsibilities are divided among multiple individuals to prevent any single person from having excessive control, thereby reducing the risk of misuse.

Identity and Access Management (IAM)

- **Overview:** IAM is a comprehensive set of processes and technologies designed to manage digital identities within an organization, ensuring that the right users have appropriate access to resources.
- **Comparison with AAA:** Both IAM and the Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting (AAA) framework are used to authenticate users, determine their access privileges, and track their activities, with each consisting of various security controls.

Key Aspects of IAM Implementation

- **Authenticating Users:** This involves verifying a user's identity through factors like knowledge (something they know), ownership (something they possess), or characteristics (something they are), often utilizing tools like Single Sign-On (SSO) and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- **User Provisioning:** This is the process of creating and maintaining a user's digital identity, including setting up accounts and configuring access rights, and conversely, deprovisioning users when their access is no longer needed.
- **Granting Authorization:** Once authenticated, authorization determines which resources a user can access, with common frameworks including Mandatory Access Control (MAC), Discretionary Access Control (DAC), and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).

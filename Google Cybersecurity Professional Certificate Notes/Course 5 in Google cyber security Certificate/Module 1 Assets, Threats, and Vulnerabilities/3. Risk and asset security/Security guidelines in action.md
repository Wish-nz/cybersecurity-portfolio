Origins and Evolution of the NIST CSF

- Originally released in 2014, the NIST CSF was developed to protect critical infrastructure in the United States, leveraging NIST's unbiased scientific data and practices.
- The framework has since been adapted to be more flexible, making it accessible for businesses of all sizes, including those with limited resources.

Components of the CSF

- The CSF consists of three main components: the **core**, which outlines desired cybersecurity outcomes across six functions (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover, Govern); **tiers**, which measure the sophistication of an organization's cybersecurity program on a scale of 1 to 4; and **profiles**, which are pre-made templates tailored to specific industry risks.
- The "Govern" function was added to the core in February 2024 to emphasize the importance of leadership in managing cybersecurity risks.

Implementing and Embracing the CSF

- The NIST CSF is a voluntary framework providing standards, guidelines, and best practices for managing cybersecurity risk, helping organizations achieve compliance with various regulations.
- While implementation can be challenging due to its detail, organizations can use guidance from the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) to create current security profiles, perform risk assessments, analyze gaps, and implement action plans.



The CIA Triad and Risk Planning

- The primary focus for security practitioners is to maintain confidentiality, integrity, and availability (the CIA triad) of assets.
- Security risk planning is the initial step in safeguarding these cornerstones, with each organization developing a unique plan based on its specific risks.

Elements of a Security Plan

- Security plans are built upon the analysis of three key elements: assets, threats, and vulnerabilities, which represent the "what," "why," and "how" of security.
- Organizations assess security risk by examining how these elements can impact the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of their information and systems.

Understanding Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities

- **Assets** are items of value to an organization, such as buildings, equipment, data, and people, requiring prioritization for protection due to limited resources.
- **Threats** are circumstances or events that can negatively affect assets, ranging from malicious actors like burglars to accidental damage from natural events.
- **Vulnerabilities** are weaknesses within an asset that can be exploited by a threat, such as a weak lock on a door or old, cracked wood, making them easy targets for attackers.

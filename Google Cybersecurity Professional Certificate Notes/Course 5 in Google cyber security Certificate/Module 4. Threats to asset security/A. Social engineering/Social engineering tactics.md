The Threat of Social Engineering

- Social engineering preys on natural human tendencies like curiosity and generosity, making it a potent threat that can bypass technological defenses.
- A notable example is the 2020 Twitter Hack, where attackers impersonated IT staff to gain network access and compromise high-profile accounts.

Common Social Engineering Tactics

- **Baiting** tempts individuals with something desirable, like an infected USB drive, to compromise their security.
- **Phishing** uses deceptive digital communications, often emails, to trick people into revealing sensitive data or installing malicious software.
- **Quid pro quo** involves promising a reward in exchange for information or access, such as a fake loan officer requesting account details for a lower interest rate.
- **Tailgating** (or piggybacking) is when an unauthorized person follows an authorized individual into a restricted area.
- **Watering hole attacks** compromise websites frequently visited by a specific group to infect users with malware.

Countering Social Engineering Threats

- Organizations can reduce risks through comprehensive security training that emphasizes staying alert to suspicious communications, being cautious about sharing information, and controlling curiosity.
- Layered defenses, including firewalls, multi-factor authentication, and email filtering, complement user awareness to protect against these attacks.

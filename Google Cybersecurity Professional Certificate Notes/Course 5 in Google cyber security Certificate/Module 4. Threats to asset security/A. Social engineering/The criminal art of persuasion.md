Understanding Social Engineering

- Social engineering involves tricking individuals into bypassing security procedures, leading to data breaches, malware infections, or unauthorized system access.
- Unlike malicious hackers who rely on programming skills, social engineers use traditional manipulation tactics, often exploiting human curiosity and willingness to help.

Stages of a Social Engineering Attack

- **Preparation:** Attackers gather information about their target to identify the best exploitation methods.
- **Establishing Trust (Pretexting):** Attackers use the gathered information to initiate communication, often disguising themselves to build a false sense of trust.
- **Persuasion:** Attackers manipulate the target into volunteering information, sometimes by using specific vocabulary to appear as an insider.
- **Disconnection:** After obtaining the desired information, attackers break communication and disappear to cover their tracks.

Preventing Social Engineering Attacks

- Implementing managerial controls, such as policies, standards, and procedures (e.g., patch management standards like NIST Special Publication 800-40), is crucial for defense.
- Staying informed about current trends and sharing knowledge about the signs of social engineering attacks with others can significantly help prevent threats.

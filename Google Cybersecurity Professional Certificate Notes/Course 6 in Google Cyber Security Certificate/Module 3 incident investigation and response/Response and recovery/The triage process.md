The Triage Process

- **Receive and Assess:** This initial step involves a security analyst receiving an alert, often from a system like an intrusion detection system (IDS). The analyst then verifies the alert's validity by gathering information and considering factors such as whether it's a false positive, if it's a recurring issue, if it's linked to a known vulnerability, and its severity.
- **Assign Priority:** Once an alert is confirmed as a genuine security issue, it's prioritized based on its potential impact, size, and scope. Key factors for prioritization include functional impact (how it affects system services), information impact (how it affects data confidentiality, integrity, and availability), and recoverability (the feasibility and cost-effectiveness of recovery).
- **Collect and Analyze:** The final step involves a comprehensive analysis of the incident. Security analysts gather evidence, conduct external research, and document the investigation to make informed decisions. Depending on the incident's severity, escalation to a level two analyst or manager may be necessary for advanced techniques.

Benefits of Triage

- **Resource Management:** Triage enables security teams to focus their resources on urgent threats, preventing the allocation of time and effort to lower-priority tasks and potentially reducing response times.
- **Standardized Approach:** Triage provides a consistent method for handling incidents. Documentation, such as playbooks, guides alerts through a structured process, ensuring that only valid alerts proceed to investigation.

Chain of Custody: Purpose and Process

- Chain of custody is a process that documents the possession and control of evidence throughout an incident's lifecycle, ensuring transparency and accountability.
- It is crucial for legal proceedings, as it helps establish the integrity, reliability, and accuracy of evidence.

Practical Application of Chain of Custody

- In digital forensics, chain of custody begins when evidence is collected, such as a compromised hard drive.
- Each transfer of the evidence between individuals must be logged in the chain of custody form, detailing who handled it, when, where, and why.

Key Elements and Consequences of a Broken Chain

- Chain of custody forms typically include a description of the evidence (e.g., hostname, IP address) and a custody log detailing transfers.
- A "broken chain of custody" occurs when there are inconsistencies or missing entries, which can compromise the evidence's trustworthiness and its admissibility in court.

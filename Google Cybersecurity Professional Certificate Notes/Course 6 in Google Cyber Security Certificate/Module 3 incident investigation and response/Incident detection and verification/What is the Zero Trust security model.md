- The Zero Trust security model is a modern approach to cybersecurity that operates on the principle of "never trust, always verify." It means that no user or device is automatically trusted, even if they are within the organization's network. Every access attempt is authenticated and authorized.

- In the video, the speaker uses an analogy of a business traveler checking into a hotel with their laptop. Historically, the system might have trusted the user because they were an employee. With Zero Trust, the system would verify the user's identity and the device's security posture before granting access to information, regardless of their location.

- This approach is a significant shift from older security models that often assumed trust once a user was inside the network perimeter.

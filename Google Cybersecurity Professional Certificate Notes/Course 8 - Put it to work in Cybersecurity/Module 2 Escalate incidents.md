# The importance of escalation

Understanding Incident Escalation

- Incident escalation is the process of identifying, triaging, and potentially handing off a security incident to a more experienced team member.
- Not all incidents require escalation, but knowing which ones do is crucial for an entry-level analyst.

Essential Skills for Escalation

- **Attention to detail:** This skill helps you quickly identify anomalies within an organization's network or systems.
- **Following escalation guidelines:** Adhering to company processes ensures proper escalation of identified issues.

Impact of Unaddressed Incidents

- Failing to escalate even minor security incidents can lead to significant problems, including financial losses, exposure of sensitive data, and reputational damage.
- Your decisions as an entry-level analyst are vital in protecting the organization's assets and data.

# Escalate with a purpose

Incident Escalation

- Security incident escalation is the process of transferring potential security incidents to a more experienced department or team member.
- As a security analyst, you'll need to recognize potential issues, such as excessive failed login attempts, and report them to the appropriate person.

Breach Notification Laws

- Many countries have breach notification laws that require companies to notify individuals of security breaches involving personally identifiable information (PII).
- PII includes sensitive customer information like Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, medical records, and addresses.

Low-Level Security Issues

- Low-level security issues are risks that do not expose PII, such as a single failed login attempt or downloading unapproved software.
- While not significant threats, these issues still require investigation to prevent potential larger impacts, like malware infections.

# Prepare to escalate through security recognition

Malware Infection

- Malware infections involve malicious software disrupting systems, ranging from simple phishing attempts to complex ransomware attacks.
- These incidents can severely impact an organization by slowing networks, blocking access to critical data, and potentially leading to financial demands.

Unauthorized Access

- Unauthorized access occurs when individuals gain unpermitted digital or physical entry to systems or applications, often through methods like brute force attacks.
- The urgency of escalating these incidents depends on the criticality of the compromised system to the organization's operations.

Improper Usage

- Improper usage involves employees violating an organization's acceptable use policies, which can be either unintentional or intentional.
- Due to the complexity of determining intent, all improper usage incidents should be escalated to a supervisor for proper handling.

# Recognize roles and responsibilities during escalation

Data-Related Roles

- **Data Owners:** These individuals have administrative control over specific information hardware or software and are accountable for its classification, protection, access, and use. They decide who can access, edit, use, or destroy their information.
- **Data Controllers:** They determine the procedures and purposes for processing data, especially customer personal information, ensuring compliance with security and privacy regulations.

Data Management and Protection

- **Data Processors:** Reporting to data controllers, these individuals or vendors are responsible for processing data and often implement security measures to protect it.
- **Data Custodians:** They are responsible for assigning and removing access to software or hardware, implementing security controls, and advising on potential threats to data.

Oversight and Compliance

- **Data Protection Officers (DPOs):** DPOs monitor internal compliance with an organization’s data protection procedures, advise the security team on obligations, and assess the effectiveness of security measures.
- **Key Takeaways for Entry-Level Analysts:** While entry-level analysts typically escalate incidents to their direct supervisor, understanding the roles of different team members is crucial for recognizing who should be reported to as they advance in their cybersecurity careers.


# From simple activity to major data breach

The Ripple Effect of Unescalated Incidents

- Even seemingly small security incidents, if left unaddressed, can lead to significant consequences for an organization.
- A delay in escalating an incident can result in data breaches, operational shutdowns, and substantial financial losses.

Incident Criticality and Urgency

- Initial incident criticality may be set to medium if there's insufficient information, and then adjusted by an experienced incident handler.
- The urgency of a security incident is determined by the assets it affects, with critical assets like manufacturing plants or PII databases requiring immediate attention.

# When and how to escalate a security incident

**Understanding Escalation Policies**

- An escalation policy is a set of actions outlining who to notify and how to handle an incident alert.
- These policies are essential for protecting the organization and its stakeholders from malicious actors.

**Navigating Challenges and Details**

- Unexpected challenges, such as a supervisor being out of office, highlight the importance of knowing your organization's escalation policy.
- Paying close attention to the small details within the policy helps in escalating incidents to the correct person and prioritizing urgency.

# Escalation timing

The Importance of Your Decisions

- Security analysts operate in a fast-paced environment, making daily decisions to safeguard company data and systems from malicious actors.
- A key part of your role involves deciding which security events to escalate before they develop into major incidents.

Trusting Your Instincts and Asking Questions

- Confidence in decision-making is crucial for a security analyst, especially during the escalation process.
- Familiarize yourself with the organization's escalation policy and don't hesitate to ask questions when needed, as this demonstrates a commitment to continuous learning.

Prioritizing Security Events

- Effective escalation requires recognizing which assets and data are most vital to your organization.
- Incidents directly impacting essential business operations should always take priority over those that do not.

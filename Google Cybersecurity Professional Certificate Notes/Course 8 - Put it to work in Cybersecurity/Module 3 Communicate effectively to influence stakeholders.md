## Stakeholders in cybersecurity

Understanding Stakeholders

- A stakeholder is any individual or group with an interest in an organization's decisions or activities.
- For an analyst, understanding stakeholders is crucial because their daily decisions impact your work, and you may need to communicate your findings to them.

Key Cybersecurity Stakeholders

- Security incidents can have significant impacts, from financial losses to data breaches, making security a top priority for various stakeholders.
- Five key stakeholders in cybersecurity include Risk Managers, the CEO, the CFO, the CISO, and Operations Managers.

Roles of Key Stakeholders

- **Risk Managers** identify and manage responses to security incidents, notify legal departments of regulatory issues, and inform public relations.
- **Operations Managers** oversee security professionals, act as a first line of defense, and are responsible for daily security operations, often working directly with analysts.

## The purpose and impact of stakeholders

Defining Stakeholders and Their Importance

- A stakeholder is any individual or group with an interest in an organization's decisions or activities.
- As a security analyst, reporting findings to various security stakeholders is a key responsibility.

Levels of Stakeholders in Organizations

- Larger organizations have many levels of stakeholders, from operations managers to high-level executives.
- While entry-level analysts may not directly communicate with all stakeholders, understanding key roles like the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and Chief Financial Officer (CFO) is important.

Communication for Entry-Level Analysts

- Entry-level analysts will likely communicate regularly with operations managers and risk managers.
- These communications involve clearly explaining security issues and their potential causes, which then inform higher-level stakeholders about the organization's overall security posture.

# Communicate for impact
## Clear and concise communication

The Importance of Tailored Communication

- When communicating sensitive information, such as security breaches, it's crucial to be mindful of what is shared and with whom.
- Different stakeholders have varying needs, so communications must be clear, concise, and focused on their specific interests.

Effective Communication Strategies

- Given that stakeholders are often busy, communications should be precise, avoid technical jargon, and have a clear purpose.
- To ensure relevance, ask managers or supervisors what information stakeholders need to know.

The Security Mindset and Stakeholder Engagement

- A key aspect of a security mindset involves understanding what matters most to stakeholders, which guides what information to share.
- Effective communication means relaying only the most relevant information to help stakeholders perform their roles effectively.

## Building blocks of cybersecurity communications

The Security Story Framework

- **Beginning, Middle, and End:** Structure your communication like a story, detailing the security challenge, its impact, and potential solutions.
- **Data-Driven:** Include relevant data such as reports or lists of issues to support your narrative.

Communicating the Story

- **Detail the Issue:** Clearly state the problem, for example, "potential malicious code execution found while monitoring the logs."
- **Reference Procedures:** Show awareness of established protocols by mentioning guidance from the organization's incident response playbook.

Delivery Methods

- **Various Channels:** Share your security story through emails, documents, visual representations, or incident management systems.
- **Visual Aids:** Utilize graphs, charts, and dashboards to convey key details and provide a pictorial representation for stakeholders.

## Communicate effectively with stakeholders

Getting to the Point in Communications

- Focus your message by asking what the stakeholder needs to know, why it's important, and when action is required.
- Explain technical situations in a non-technical manner to ensure understanding.

Communicating with Impact

- Tailor your communication to the specific stakeholder's role and interests, such as daily operations for operations managers or potential financial impact for senior-level stakeholders.
- Concentrate on information relevant to their responsibilities to effectively convey crucial details.

Choosing Communication Methods

- Select the appropriate communication channel based on the complexity of the message, such as instant messaging for straightforward updates or email for complex situations.
- Consider using visual aids like graphs for data-heavy information to enhance clarity.

## Visual storytelling in cybersecurity

Utilizing Visuals for Impact

- Visuals like charts and graphs can effectively communicate data and metrics, helping to tell a security story.
- Detailed and relevant graphics can assist stakeholders in making informed decisions to protect the organization.

Choosing the Right Communication Method

- For sensitive information, email or phone calls may be more appropriate, but always follow organizational procedures and ensure correct recipients.
- When emails are not promptly answered, a phone call or instant message can be more effective for urgent issues, demonstrating initiative.

Showcasing Communication Skills

- Visual representations, emails, and phone calls are opportunities to highlight written and verbal communication abilities.
- Following up with stakeholders demonstrates initiative and can prevent major issues.

## Create visual dashboards for impactful cybersecurity communications

The Importance of Visual Communication

- Clear and concise communication is crucial in cybersecurity, especially when dealing with busy stakeholders who have other responsibilities.
- Visuals help provide decision-makers with actionable information, enabling them to quickly identify and address potential security risks.

Visual Dashboards for Storytelling

- Visual dashboards are tools that display various types of data in one place, making them ideal for communicating security events involving numbers and data.
- Dashboards can range from simple, containing a single chart, to complex, including multiple detailed charts, graphs, and tables, depending on the information being conveyed.

When to Use Visuals

- Visual communication is particularly effective when presenting data-driven information, such as audit results or trends, to stakeholders.
- Instead of lengthy descriptions, visuals like graphs and charts can clearly illustrate findings, making them easier for stakeholders to understand quickly.
